A neighbor down the block, Larry Lang, was an engineer at Hewlett-Packard, and he spent a lot of time teaching me electronics. He built Heathkits: you bought the product as a kit, paid more than you would for the finished thing, and put it together yourself from a manual with every part laid out and color coded. It taught me what was inside a finished product and how it worked. More than that, it taught me I could build the things I saw around me.

"These things were not mysteries anymore." (Smithsonian oral history, 1995)
