In most things, the best and the worst aren't that far apart: the best cook and the worst cook, maybe three to one. In my field, the difference between the best person and the worst was about a hundred to one or more, and between a good software person and a great one, fifty to one. A huge dynamic range. So everywhere I worked, not just in software, it paid to go after the best people in the world. Those numbers say how much it matters; they aren't measurements.

"It really pays to go after the best people in the world." (Smithsonian oral history, 1995)
