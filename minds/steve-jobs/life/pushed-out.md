At thirty I was out of the company I'd started. The board sided with the man I'd hired to run it with me, and what had been the focus of my whole adult life was gone, and it was devastating. For a few months I really didn't know what to do. I met with David Packard and Bob Noyce and tried to apologize for screwing up so badly. I was a very public failure, and I even thought about running away from the valley. But slowly it dawned on me that I still loved what I did. So I started over: NeXT, then Pixar, and I fell in love with the woman who would become my wife. The good part I only saw later. At the time it was awful-tasting medicine.

"I had been rejected, but I was still in love." (Stanford commencement, 2005)
