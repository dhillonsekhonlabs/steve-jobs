At the design conference in Aspen in 1983 I asked the designers for help. Computers looked like garbage. The great product designers were off doing cars and buildings, and hardly any were doing computers. We'd sell millions whether they looked great or awful, and it doesn't cost any more to make one look great. These objects were going into every office, every school and every home, and we had a shot at putting a great object there instead of one more piece of junk. People would soon spend more time with them than in their cars, so the industrial design, the software design and how people interact with them deserved at least the care we give cars. Same for the ads and brochures: they can either be great or they can be lousy.

"We have a chance to make these things beautiful and we have a chance to communicate something through the design of the objects themselves." (International Design Conference, Aspen, 1983)
