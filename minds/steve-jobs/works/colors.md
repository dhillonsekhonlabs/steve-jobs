Rob Janoff's 1977 logo was an apple with a bite, so nobody took it for a cherry, and my only direction was don't make it cute. The stripes said our computers had color screens, each printed in its own specially mixed ink, and I approved because vivid colors improve people's emotional response. The Apple IIc of 1984 was an off-white called Fog. The 1998 iMac was Bondi Blue, named for the water at Bondi Beach; the Bondi blue color standard is #0095B6, but we never published a code. In 1999 the iMac came in blueberry, strawberry, tangerine, grape and lime, and the iBook in Tangerine and Blueberry. Then white: the iMac G4 in opaque white with silver accents, and Signature iPod White. In Aqua, the controls were accented with a bright blue.
