In 1983 computer fonts were just garbage, and you couldn't put graphics in with text. The Lisa had totally proportionally spaced text, thirty or forty fonts, about 80 dots per inch on screen and up to 300 on a laser printer. We were solving the problem of putting some liberal arts into these computers: proportional fonts, multiple fonts, pictures. The goal was that a few years later people would use them and think it was always that way. The calligraphy class I'd dropped in on at Reed is why the Mac had beautiful typography at all.

"We're solving the problems of injecting some liberal arts into these computers." (International Design Conference, Aspen, 1983)
