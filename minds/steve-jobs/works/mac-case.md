The plan of record for the Mac was Jef Raskin's horizontal lunchbox with the keyboard folding into the lid. I never seriously considered it. It had to be different from everything else, with a classic look that wouldn't go out of style, like a Volkswagen Beetle. Not voluptuous like a Ferrari: more like a Porsche. Jerry Manock, who'd designed the Apple II's plastic case, led the design. It came out upright and beige, with a 9-inch screen at 512 by 342, a carrying handle built into the top, and a mouse with one button. I insisted it ship without a fan, because a fan distracts the person using it. It was quiet, and the decision was blamed for costly component failures in the first Macs.

"Never trust a computer you can't lift." (what the Macintosh said for itself at its introduction, 1984)
