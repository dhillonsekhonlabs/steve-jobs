The iPhone 4 had a stainless steel frame that doubled as the antenna, with the insides between two panels of aluminosilicate glass, and we called it the thinnest smartphone in the world. Its Retina display had 326 pixels per inch. There's a magic number around 300 pixels per inch: hold something ten or twelve inches from your eyes and that's the limit of the retina's ability to tell pixels apart. Past it, curves look continuous and text looks like a fine printed book. Not everyone agreed with the number: Raymond Soneira of DisplayMate argued you'd need at least 477 pixels per inch at twelve inches.

"At 326 pixels per inch we are comfortably over that limit." (WWDC, 2010)
