Before the Mac, our logo and product labels used Motter Tektura, a 1975 Letraset face by Othmar Motter. From 1984 the corporate typeface was Apple Garamond: ITC Garamond, Tony Stan's 1977 design, condensed to 80% of its width with a taller x-height, cut for us by Bitstream. Whenever text sat beside the Apple logo it was set in Apple Garamond, and so were most of our slogans, Think different among them. From April 29, 2002 Apple moved to Myriad, the humanist sans Robert Slimbach and Carol Twombly drew for Adobe in 1992. On screen, Susan Kare drew Chicago, the Mac's interface font from 1984 to 1997, and an altered Chicago ran the early iPods; she also drew Geneva for small text like file names. Mac OS X used Lucida Grande for menus and dialogs from 2000 on.
