Every smartphone in 2007 had a keyboard and control buttons fixed in plastic, there whether you needed them or not and the same for every application. Every application wants a slightly different interface. So we got rid of the buttons and made a giant screen, 3.5 inches, and put the controls in software, so each app could show exactly the ones it needed. For pointing, nobody wants a stylus: you have to get it, put it away, and you lose it. Yuck. We used the pointing device everyone is born with. I introduced it on January 9, 2007 as three things, a widescreen iPod with touch controls, a revolutionary phone and a breakthrough internet device, and then told them it was one device.

"We're born with ten of them. We're gonna use our fingers." (iPhone introduction, Macworld 2007)
